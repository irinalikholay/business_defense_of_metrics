## Business Claim 

The business observes that Average Order Value (AOV) has increased and concludes that customers are spending more per order.

## Why This Claim Is Risky 

An increase in Average Order Value does not necessarily mean that most customers are spending more.
AOV can be heavily influenced by a small number of high-value orders, which may distort the overall picture of customer behavior.

## Business Risk 

If the company relies solely on AOV:
 - pricing decisions may be incorrect
 - marketing performance may be misinterpreted
 - customer value may be overestimated

 This can lead to poor strategic and operational decision.

 ## Distribution Reality Chech

 To validate whether AOV reflects typical customer behavior, we analyzed the distribution of order values.

 Key observations:
  - The median order value is significantly lower than the mean (AOV)
  - 75 % of orders are below a relatively low threshold
  - Only a small fraction of orders reach very high values

This indicates a highly right-skewed distribution , where most customers spend far less then the reported average.

## Who Breaks the Metric

To understand why the Average Order Value (AOV) is misleading,
we analyzed how revenue is distributed across different order groups.

Orders were sorted by value, and the contribution of the top 1%, 5%, and 10% of orders
to total revenue was calculated.

The results show that a very small fraction of high-value orders generates
a disproportionately large share of total revenue.

This means that the average order value is heavily influenced by rare,
expensive orders and does not represent the typical customer behavior.

Business risk:
Decisions based solely on AOV may lead to incorrect pricing, marketing,
and customer segmentation strategies.


## Better Metrics

Since the Average Order Value is distorted by high-value outliers,
alternative metrics were evaluated to better represent typical customer behavior.

The following metrics were analyzed:
- Median Order Value
- 75th, 90th, and 95th percentiles
- Trimmed Average Order Value (excluding the top 5% of orders)

The median order value provides a more stable measure of a typical order,
as it is not affected by extreme values.

Percentiles help identify thresholds for high-value customers and pricing strategies.

The trimmed average order value offers a compromise between the mean and median,
reflecting the average behavior of the majority of customers.

Recommendation:
For decision-making and reporting, the median order value should be used
alongside percentiles and trimmed averages rather than relying solely on AOV.
